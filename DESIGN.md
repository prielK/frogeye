# FrogEye Design System

Recorded from the shipped frontend (`frontend/src/styles/globals.css` and the components under `frontend/src`). Where the build and the original seed disagreed, the build is recorded and the divergence is noted.

## 1. Overview: The Morning Edition

**Creative North Star.** A morning briefing that happens to be a tool. Headlines read like the FT or Economist Espresso (a serif voice, tight measure, plain hierarchy); everything around them behaves like the Stripe Dashboard (flat, aligned, calm). In the build this lands as the **Morning Briefing** (the home page and flagship: an editorial front page per edition, `features/briefing`), a document feed of serif story titles with sans metadata, the **Signals Explorer** (every signal, ranked and faceted, `features/signals`), the **Competitors** list (who matters to JFrog and who is moving: the week's biggest moves, the landscape and every competitor in one table, `features/competitors`) and each competitor's **deep dive** (`/competitors/:slug`: one long page, thirteen sections, a source and a date for every number), the **Products** list and each JFrog product's **deep dive** (`/products`, `/products/:slug`, `features/products`: who presses the product and how hard, its gaps and what each team should do, with no JFrog company figure), the **Compare** matrix (`/compare`, `features/compare`: JFrog's products against every competitor's on one screen, each pairing one click from its evidence), a quiet admin console of tables for sources and ingestion runs, and the **Agent runs** pages (`features/admin`: every daily agent run, its step trace, and the AI spend against both budget caps) and **Evals** (`/admin/evals`: how accurate the models and guards are, measured on labelled cases). Every signal has a **full analysis page**, `/signals/:id` (`features/analysis`): the story's cited analysis, read like an editorial long-read; every Briefing story opens it directly. Every page shares one quick-look signal drawer (`components/signal`), opened by `?signal=<id>` (from the Signals list, Search everything and the breaking toast), which links to that page.

**Theme.** Light by default, dark by toggle (`ThemeToggle`, `lib/theme.ts`; the `.dark` class on `html` flips the same token names, `color-scheme` follows). The theme switch crossfades for 220ms via view transitions and is skipped under reduced motion. Dark mode adds `letter-spacing: 0.006em` on body because light-on-dark text reads thinner.

**The Calm Instrument Rule.** Hierarchy, not decoration, tells the reader where to look. The only saturated fills in the interface are the primary button, soft status badges, the funnel, the meters and the chart kit's thin marks (lines, dots, bars; large areas are tints). Chrome (shell, tables, forms) stays tonal. In the Briefing, structure comes from rules, not containers: a 1px ink rule (`border-foreground`) starts each section, neutral hairlines (`border`) separate the items inside it. A story's hover wash is a state, not a container: nothing boxes a card at rest.

**The Evidence Within Reach Rule.** Every feed row shows its source name, relative publication time, the companies mentioned (clickable chips that filter), and a "N matched terms" control whose tooltip answers "Why it's in the feed". Funnel bars carry a tooltip with pass rates and dropped duplicates. Every briefing takeaway and story shows its Admiralty grade and trust state (the trust line), its companies and its outlet by name; one click on the card or row opens its full analysis page, where every factual item carries numbered citations into a Sources list (a story without a full analysis shows its stage-A verified quotes instead), and the side column says why it ranks where it does. Elsewhere (the Signals list, Search everything, the breaking toast) one click opens the signal drawer's quick look (summary, actions, first sources) and one more the page. Trust is on the row or one hover away.

**Motion: Responsive.** Color/border transitions run 150ms, small popovers 100ms, dialogs and alerts 200ms, with `ease-out-quart` (`cubic-bezier(.25,1,.5,1)`) for controls and `ease-out-expo` (`cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)`) for entering elements. Buttons press down 1px. `prefers-reduced-motion` collapses all animation and transition durations to 1ms.

**Layout.** Desktop-first shell: 56px sticky top bar, 240px sidebar (hidden below `md`, opened as a sheet), main content capped at 76rem with 16/24/40px side padding by breakpoint; the Morning Briefing (`data-wide` on its article) and the Compare matrix (`data-wide` on its page), read by `has-[[data-wide]]` in AppShell, run to 112rem, so they use the width of the screen. Prose measure: page descriptions 65ch, excerpts 75ch.

**Dates and times.** One helper set (`lib/time`, numbers and percents in `lib/format`) renders every date and time in the app's time zone, the backend's TIMEZONE delivered on the signed-in user, so a reader abroad sees the same "today" as the briefing, the budget and the cost chart. Absolute times name the zone in their tooltip ("Mon 19 Oct 2026, 01:25 (Israel Time)"); plain dates (a briefing's, a cost day's) are calendar days and never shift. Tests run under `TZ=UTC` to keep it so.

## 2. Colors: The Restrained Palette

**The Restrained Rule.** Neutrals are tinted toward hue 145 at chroma 0.005 to 0.015; JFrog green (#40BE46, `oklch(0.707 0.195 143.9)`) is an accent only. Tailwind's default palette is removed (`--color-*: initial`), so components can only use tokens.

- **Surfaces (light):** `background` L0.985, `card` and `popover` L0.997, `sidebar` L0.965, `secondary`/`muted`/`accent` L0.94 to 0.955. Hairline `border` L0.905; form `input` border L0.84, darkening to L0.74 on hover.
- **Text:** `foreground` L0.2; `muted-foreground` L0.47 (light) / L0.71 (dark).
- **Primary green:** `primary` is the same in both themes and used for the primary button fill, the wordmark eye, and the selected-persona avatar. Text and icons in green use `primary-ink` (L0.47 light, L0.78 dark) so they stay legible; `primary-soft` is the tinted badge/selection ground; `ring` is the focus color. Button text on green is a dark green ink (`primary-foreground`), not white.
- **Meaning:** `threat` (red, 27), `opportunity` (blue, 252), `neutral` (grey-green), `warning` (amber, 68/80), each with a `-soft` ground. `destructive` equals `threat`. Used as soft-fill badge pairs and error text; always accompanied by a word or icon.
- **Funnel ramp:** four ordinal steps in the brand hue (`funnel-1..4`), lightness monotone from recessive to strongest in light mode and reversed anchor in dark (first stage closest to ground, last glows). Used only by `FunnelBar`; it means "pipeline stage" and is not reused for other bars.
- **Meter:** `meter` (fill) and `meter-track` (track) show one value against its maximum: the analysis page's "Why ranked here" bars, the Signals Explorer's importance meter (also validated at >= 3:1 on the page `background`: 4.0:1 light, 6.6:1 dark), the Agent runs budget meters, the trace's cost-share meters and the 7-day spend columns. The fill is L0.57 light / L0.66 dark in the brand hue (dataviz-validated as a mark: 4.15:1 and 6.20:1 on `card`, inside the lightness band); the track equals the hairline `border`, a reference extent whose value is always printed beside it.
- **Meter severity (budget meters only):** a cap meter's fill turns `warning` from 80% of the cap and `threat` at the cap, always beside the words "Near the cap" / "Cap reached" and their icon. Validated as marks: light `warning` 5.62:1 on `card` / 5.43:1 on `background`, `threat` 6.04:1 / 5.83:1; dark `warning` 9.56:1 / 10.13:1, `threat` 6.80:1 / 7.20:1; every fill clears 3:1 against `meter-track` (light >= 3.16:1, dark >= 4.94:1). Severity never colours a series: the 7-day columns stay `meter` and the cap is a reference line.
- **Chart series** (`chart-1..5`, the chart kit, §5 Charts). Four categorical slots in a fixed order, **green 145, teal 210, magenta 325, violet 285**, assigned in sequence and never cycled, plus `chart-5`, the recessive grey that means "Other", and a sparkline's recessive line (its accent dot wears `chart-1`). The hues keep clear of the meaning tokens, so a series never reads as threat (red 27), opportunity (blue 252) or warning (amber 68): the nearest slot is 15.0+ OKLab ΔE from any of them in either theme. Each theme is selected, not flipped. Dataviz-validated on `card` (light L0.997, dark L0.2): every slot in the band with chroma >= 0.10; all four pairs CVD-safe (worst protan/deutan ΔE 12.8 light, 8.5 dark; target 8) and apart under normal vision (worst ΔE 15.7 light, 16.8 dark; floor 15), so even the scatter's all-pairs test passes with four slots. As marks, light `chart-1..5` measure 4.15 / 3.32 / 6.38 / 4.02 / 3.89:1 on `card` and 4.01 / 3.21 / 6.16 / 3.89 / 3.76:1 on `background`; dark 6.20 / 4.57 / 5.44 / 3.60 / 4.24:1 on `card` and 6.57 / 4.84 / 5.77 / 3.82 / 4.50:1 on `background` (popover >= 3.48:1). `chart-1` equals `meter`, the app's one-value hue. The retune replaced green-blue-amber-red-grey: light `chart-1` was the raw brand green at 2.41:1, `chart-2..4` were the opportunity, warning and threat values themselves, and the red/amber pair collapsed under deuteranopia (ΔE 1.1). `charts.test.tsx` guards the 3:1 floor and the distance from the meaning tokens in both themes. That distance holds for **normal colour vision only**: simulated at full severity (Machado 2009), `chart-1` comes within ΔE 4.2 of `threat` and 5.1 of `warning`, and `chart-3` within 4.8 of `opportunity`, for deuteranopia in the light theme (dark: 7.0 to 8.4), so colour-blind readers tell a meaning from a series by its words and its position (the side of the baseline, the label beside it, the icon), never by hue; a meaning token never shares a plot with categorical series.
- **Overlay and selection:** `overlay` is a tinted translucent scrim (0.36 alpha light, 0.6 dark); `::selection` uses a soft green.

**Active navigation** is a tonal `sidebar-accent` fill with a `primary-ink` icon, not a green fill.

## 3. Typography: Editorial Display, Precise Body

Self-hosted via `@fontsource-variable` (no CDN): **Source Serif 4 Variable** (optical-size axis) for the headline voice and **Schibsted Grotesk Variable** for everything else. Metric-matched local fallbacks (`Arial` and `Georgia` with size-adjust and ascent overrides) prevent reflow on font swap. Mono is the system stack, used for `code/kbd/pre/samp`.

**The Four Sizes Rule.** The whole app uses four sizes, fixed rem, no fluid sizing, defined once in the `@theme` block of `globals.css`:

| Token | Size / line height | Face | Carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| `display` | 32 / 40 (2rem / 2.5rem, -0.012em) | serif | Page titles (`PageHeader`), the Briefing's headline, the analysis page's title, the login headline, the Briefing's empty-state titles that stand in for its headline |
| `title` | 20 / 28 (1.25rem / 1.75rem) | serif | Card and row headlines: every takeaway title, story one-liners, Signals Explorer and Search everything row titles, Feed titles, the signal drawer's title, the analysis page's and the drawer quick look's section heads, the "Morning Briefing" nameplate, the "last full briefing" link |
| `body` | 15 / 24 (0.9375rem / 1.5rem) | sans | All running text and every control: the body default, the Briefing lead, card summaries, why-it-matters lines and actions (with their semibold labels), "Read full analysis →", KPI figures such as the importance number (semibold, tabular), summaries, excerpts, page descriptions, buttons, inputs, selects, tabs, menus, nav, table cells, section heads and card or dialog titles (semibold), the analysis page's side-column heads (semibold) |
| `meta` | 13 / 18 (0.8125rem / 1.125rem) | sans | Labels, chips, badges, the grade badge, tooltips, table headers, timestamps and datelines, trust lines, the funnel strip, kbd hints, mono arguments and error panels, small print |

No other size exists: no other `--text-*` token, no arbitrary `text-[…]` size, no Tailwind default size (`text-sm`, `text-lg`…), and responsive or state variants (`sm:text-…`) use only the four names. `lib/utils.test.ts` scans every source file and fails on anything else, naming the file and the class. The only exceptions are the two graphics (allowlisted as exact file-and-class pairs): the wordmark (`text-[1.625rem]` large, `text-[1.1875rem]`) and the `CompetitorChip` monogram (`text-[0.5625rem]`). Third-party components that inject their own unlayered CSS are pinned to the scale with Tailwind's important suffix (`text-body!`, `text-meta!`): sonner's toasts would otherwise render at 13px and their action buttons at 12px.

**Hierarchy within a size.** Size does not carry rank; weight (400 / 500 / 600), colour (`foreground`, `muted-foreground`, `primary-ink`, the meaning tokens, all contrast-checked in both themes), spacing and the voice do. So a section head is body semibold on an ink rule above body muted text (the analysis page and the drawer quick look set theirs in the serif at `title`); an empty state's title is body medium in `foreground` over a muted hint; a card title is body semibold over a muted description. Every card in a list has the same anatomy and sizes: the lead takeaway spans more width (and, from `xl`, a ruled column for its "What to do"), never a larger size. One size at every width: a display headline over 90 characters takes a 40ch measure instead of 30ch, it does not shrink.

**The Two Voices Rule.** The serif utility `font-headline` (weight 600) speaks only at `display` and `title` (plus the wordmark): page titles, the login headline, the Briefing's headline, nameplate, takeaway titles, story one-liners, the "last full briefing" link and empty-state titles, the signal drawer's title and section heads, the analysis page's title and section heads, and the Signals Explorer and Feed row titles. Nothing at `body` or `meta` is serif: tables, forms, buttons, navigation, tabs, section heads, badges and metadata are Schibsted Grotesk. Headlines are never truncated: long ones wrap with `text-wrap: balance` at a capped measure.

**Inputs at 15px.** Inputs use `body` at every width, so iOS Safari zooms into any focused field below 16px: sign-in, and the Signals, Feed and Sources search boxes. Accepted for every phone input: FrogEye is a desktop-first internal tool, and phones get a readable briefing, not a form-filling one.

**Numerals.** Tabular lining numerals are applied only to marked data (`<data>`, `<kbd>`, `[data-numeric]`, via `components/common/Num`), not to whole tables or running text, because the face's tabular punctuation widens separators. `Num` takes a `format` for figures other than counts (money from `formatUsd`, compact token counts "182K" from `formatCompact`, both in `lib/format`), so "$0.62" or "224.5K" keeps tabular digits with proportional "." and ","; never put `data-numeric` on a mixed string ("$0 . 62", "2 LLM calls ," is the failure).

**Money.** `formatUsd`: two decimals by default ("$0.62", "<$0.01" under half a cent); `precise` keeps two significant digits under a dollar for per-call and per-run amounts ("$0.018", "$0.000041"); `decimals` fixes the places in a column so it lines up (the runs table's cost: 3, "$0.104"; the trace ledger: 4, "$0.0040", "<$0.0001").

Headings use `text-wrap: balance`; paragraphs use `text-wrap: pretty`.

## 4. Elevation

Flat by default: tone plus a 1px `border` separates cards, sidebar, tables and list rows. One shadow token, `shadow-floating` (`0 12px 32px -12px` plus `0 2px 6px -2px`, green-tinted ink; near-black in dark), is used on dialogs, the skip link, and other floating layers. Tooltips invert (foreground ground, background text) and take no shadow.

**Z-index scale** (CSS variables, used as `z-(--z-*)`): dropdown 10, sticky 20, modal-backdrop 30, modal 40, popover 50, toast 60, tooltip 70. `dropdown` is for in-flow non-portaled layers; portaled menus, selects, popovers and the command palette sit on `popover` so they clear the sticky header and any open dialog or sheet. Dialog overlay is on `modal-backdrop`, dialog content on `modal`, tooltips on `tooltip`; the top bar uses `sticky`. The signal drawer is a right-hand Sheet on the same tiers (scrim on `modal-backdrop`, panel on `modal`, `shadow-floating`), so tooltips inside it and toasts (`toast`) stay above it. `ui/layering.test.tsx` guards this ordering.

## 5. Components

Restyled shadcn primitives in `components/ui`, plus a few domain components.

- **Button.** Radius 6px, `body` medium text (`meta` at size xs), 150ms color transition, 2px ring focus with offset. Variants: default (green fill, dark ink text), outline (card ground, input border), secondary, ghost, destructive (threat fill), link (`primary-ink`). Sizes: xs 24, sm 32, default 36, lg 40, plus square icon sizes. `loading` shows a spinner and sets `aria-busy`.
- **Input.** 36px, 6px radius, card ground, `input` border that deepens on hover; `body` text at every width.

Notes follow as icon-plus-title blocks: what went wrong (the error in a mono panel), AI analysis was off, stopped at the budget cap, and stage errors listed in mono.
- **Step timeline** (`StepTimeline`): node, then agent, then steps, in execution order. Each **node row** is a disclosure and a line of the run's cost ledger: a chevron, the stage's name (the live-run pill's activity labels: "Triaging documents"), its agents and kinds beneath ("Analyst, Embeddings · 9 LLM calls, 9 tool calls, 3 notes"; a step without an agent is labelled by its node's agent where one is known, else "Pipeline" for the graph's own notes), then right-aligned columns: steps, tokens, a 4px x 40px **cost-share meter** (the node's share of the run's cost) and its cost to 4 places. ### Charts (`components/charts`: the chart kit)

One in-house kit of SVG charts on three pinned d3 modules (`d3-scale` 4.0.2, `d3-shape` 3.2.0, `d3-array` 3.2.4, exact versions) for the competitor and product pages: `ChartFrame`, `LineChart`, `StackedArea`, `BarList`, `StepLine`, `DotTimeline`, `Swimlane`, `BubbleScatter`, `Sparkline`, `DivergingBars`, with `scales` (colour roles, shapes, dates, ticks, the fold, width, pointer and keyboard) and `tooltip`. A chart earns its place by answering its section's question; it is never one of a grid of charts competing for the eye.

- **The frame** (`ChartFrame`). Every chart is a `<figure>` named by its title, its `figcaption` first: the title (`body` semibold, what is measured: "Open roles"), then the **printed one-line summary** (`body` muted, the takeaway: "142 open now, up 12 in 30 days"), so the so-what is read before the plot. Every chart requires `title`, `summary` and `empty`; controls that scope one chart (a 7/30/90 toggle) sit top right, under the caption on phones. Then the legend, the plot, a footnote (`meta` muted, 72ch: method, what is left out, "Reported figures only — no line is drawn between them") and the **table twin**: "Show the numbers", a `<details>` table of every value the chart draws, exactly (folded series unfolded; figures right, words left).
- **Empty state.** No data draws no axes and no zero line: the frame keeps its title and swaps the summary and plot for the app's `EmptyState` (dashed panel, title plus a hint that teaches how it fills: "No GitHub stars recorded" / "FrogEye reads its 2 repositories once a day…"). It is for a chart that is empty inside a section that has something else to show; a deep-dive section with nothing of its own is one quiet line instead (§5 Competitor deep dive). A chart is empty when it has nothing to draw (no series with points, no steps, no figures and no events, no lane events in the window, every period zero for `DivergingBars`). `Sparkline` says it in words, named by its `label`: null is "Insufficient data" (not enough evidence to measure), [] is "No data yet" (measured, nothing recorded). Nothing shows 0 as if it were measured.
- **Colour: four categorical colours plus a neutral "Other", and no more.** Marks wear only `chart-1..5`, the four meaning tokens and the meter pair (guarded by `charts.test.tsx`); chrome wears the neutral tokens (gridlines `border`, baselines `input`, labels `muted-foreground`, direct labels `foreground`). A named series takes one of `chart-1..4` (`SeriesColor`), in the order given, so colour follows the entity and callers keep the order stable; an explicit `color` pins a slot. `maxSeries` is 1 to 4 (`SeriesCap`; anything larger is four): a fifth category or more folds into **"Other (n)"** in `chart-5`, summed per x (the top four by total), and a named series never wears the "Other" grey. A caller may build its Other itself, a series with `members` whose points are what to draw (the product page's hiring chart: its members' sum from the first day all of them are measured): the kit draws it as its own Other, dashed in `chart-5` outside the named cap, counts and names its members in the legend ("Other (2)", their names on hover) and unfolds them in the table twin. Share of voice is therefore the competitor plus its top 3 peers by overlap. `BarList` folds past `max` rows (default 8, never folding a single row); its rows are one series, slot 1 for every bar (a row that means something may wear its meaning token, `BarItem.color`: the Briefing's stance rows). The meaning tokens appear only where the chart means them (`DivergingBars`' opportunity above and threat below); a series is never "red" because it is the fifth. Text never wears a series colour.
- **Nothing is colour alone, at any width.** Each slot has a marker shape, **circle, square, triangle, diamond** (`SERIES_SHAPES`), and "Other" is the **dashed** line or edge (`OTHER_DASH`). A multi-series `LineChart` puts its shape on every point where they are 44px apart or more, else on a regular sample that keeps them 44px apart, and always on the last point; a `StackedArea` puts it along each layer's top edge and names every band at least 16px tall inside itself at the right edge, on a surface halo. The legend keys and the tooltip keys repeat the same line, tint, shape and dash, so a crossing can be followed at 390px without hue. Up to four lines are also named at their ends when they end together, fit (from 480px), do not collide and every name fits the 132px end gutter; a longer name (an `org/repo`) leaves every end unlabelled rather than clipped or run past the plot, and the legend names every series. State is shape, position or a word: a **hollow solid ring** is "Sources disagree" (`DotTimeline` figures, `StepLine` rounds); a **hollow dashed ring** or outline is a value that is not known (an undisclosed amount, a bubble with no size: "No job board"); a triangle along the top is an event; opportunity is the side above the baseline with its word and an arrow; a `BarList` change is a drawn arrow plus its sign ("+3", "−2"); bubble groups differ in shape as well as colour.
- **Marks.** Lines 2px with round joins (no smoothing: straight segments between measured points), 1.5px in a sparkline; markers r4 (r3.5 on a stack's edge, r5.5 when active) with a 1.5px ring in the surface colour (`surface="card"` on a card); point dots r4.5–6 with a 2px ring; a soft 10% wash under a single line; stacked layers a 20% tint edged by a 1.5px line; bars and columns at most 24px (8px for `BarList`), 4px rounded at the data end, square at the baseline; bubbles a 45% fill with a 1.5px edge, solid when active. Gridlines are solid hairlines, never dashed: a dash means "Other" or "not known". Value axes start at zero by default and counts get whole ticks only (no "0.5 postings"); ticks compact ("1.2K", "$80M"), exact values in tooltips, labels and the table. A short series (up to six days or weeks: a young deployment's few collected weeks, a young board) is labelled at its own measured days, never at regular ticks between them, and a single day is its axis's only tick: the scale pads a day on each side for room, but no tick names a day nothing was measured on, tomorrow least (`timeTicks`; `DotTimeline` keeps its regular grid except for one day's figures). Chart geometry never transitions (`transition: none` inside every plot), so a resize or a capture never shows marks between two layouts.
- **Honest totals and sizes.** `StepLine` adds only stated amounts: from the first undisclosed step on, the total is a lower bound, "≥ $22M", the line is the "Disclosed total" and the end label reads "≥ $87M disclosed"; when no step states an amount there is no money axis, no total line and no end label, only the time axis, the dashed rings and the words "Amounts not disclosed" (the table's total column reads "—"). Each step's own column sits just left of the total's riser, so the rise never splits it. `BubbleScatter` draws a point with no size (`size.missingLabel`, "No job board") as a hollow dashed neutral outline at a fixed size in its group's shape, named in the legend, tooltip and table; never as a small filled bubble, because area is the measure. A point may name its own reason (`missingLabel` on the point: "First snapshot pending" beside "No job board"), said in its tooltip, accessible name and table row, while the legend names the shared mark ("No open-roles count yet"). The legend lists only the groups drawn, and a group keeps the slot its place in `groups` gives it, so a filter that empties a group never repaints the others. `BarList` scales to its largest **named** row, so a big "Other" never shrinks the rows that matter; an "Other" larger than that is cut short by two slanted gaps masked out of the bar near its end (a real gap, so a row's hover wash shows through it, never strokes painted in the surface colour), its exact value printed and its tooltip saying so.
- **Tooltip** (`tooltip`). Every mark shows its exact values on hover and on keyboard focus alike: a popover surface (`popover`, hairline `border`, `shadow-floating`, 16rem at most, `meta`), the day or name first, then rows that lead with the value (semibold) and follow with the label (muted), keyed like the legend. Line and area charts snap a crosshair to the nearest day and list every series there (a stacked chart adds its total under a hairline); their tooltip sits **beside** the crosshair at the plot's top (right of it in the left half, left of it in the right half), so it never covers the column of points. Every other tooltip sits above its mark and, near the top of the plot, flips below it **hanging from the mark's bottom edge**, so it never covers the mark or its label. It enhances, never gates: the summary, labels and the table twin carry the values too, and each mark's accessible name is the tooltip's text.
- **Pointer and keyboard.** Line, area and column charts take the pointer at plot level: the whole plot is the hit target and the nearest day or period wins (no thin per-day strips), with `touch-action: pan-y` on the plot's wrapping element (`PLOT_TOUCH`; iOS Safari ignores it on an SVG child), so a finger scrubs sideways and the page still scrolls; the plot's left edge is read once per hover, and again after a scroll or a resize moves it. On touch a readout stays up after the finger lifts (a pointerleave follows the pointerup there) until the next tap anywhere, and a tap on the plot moves it; a mouse that leaves hides it at once (`useTouchHold`). Dots, steps and bubbles have hit circles of at least 24px; `BarList` rows are their full row. One tab stop per chart (roving tabindex): the arrow keys walk the marks in reading order, Home and End jump to the ends, **Escape hides the tooltip and keeps focus on the point** (the next arrow key shows it again); focus draws a 2px `ring` (a ring round the dot or bubble, the crosshair, or the column outline). A mark that leads somewhere (a swimlane dot to its analysis, a bubble to its profile) is a real link; a plain click goes through the caller's `onNavigate`, modified clicks open a tab.
- **Responsive.** Each chart reads its width synchronously before the first paint and then follows it with a ResizeObserver, so a phone never paints a frame at the fallback width. Tick counts thin with the width and edge ticks anchor inward, end labels drop below 480px (the shapes, the legend and the in-band names stay), bubble and lane-label sizes shrink, `BarList`'s label column grows with the chart's own width (a container query: 9rem, 11rem from a 32rem chart, 15rem from 42rem), and a label that still does not fit truncates with its full name on hover. No chart overflows a 390px phone.
- **Forms.** `LineChart` (change over time, 1–4 named series), `StackedArea` (part-to-whole over time), `BarList` (magnitude across categories, with an optional signed change; `extent` fixes the scale, a rate's 1: bars are drawn against it rather than the largest named row and the tooltip drops "of all shown", which a rate does not have; `BarItem.aside` is a quiet `meta` word after the value, "9/22", the cases a rate counts, repeated in the tooltip and the table twin), `StepLine` (a running total in steps, funding: the total as a step line, each step's own amount as a column on the same axis), `DotTimeline` (reported figures only: a dot per figure and never a path; same-day figures side by side; events off the value axis as triangles along the top), `Swimlane` (moves per lane on one time axis, every lane printed, dot size is importance), `BubbleScatter` (two measures and a third as area, groups of up to four, reference lines and quadrant captions placed clear of the bubbles: each caption tries its places in turn, a reference line's label right of the plot above the line, then below it, then at the left, and is left out when none is free), `Sparkline` (a table cell's 13-week trend, grey line, accent end dot, one tab stop; the cell prints the value beside it), `DivergingBars` (two opposing measures per period on one symmetric scale). Never two value axes on one plot.

A written day lays out in two columns from `xl`: the stories (`minmax(0,1fr)`), and an 18rem right rail (21rem from `2xl`, 48/64px gutter) that starts beside the lede and stays in view (`sticky`, 88px from the top) holding **Today at a glance**. Below `xl` the glance is a strip between the lede and the stories, its three lists abreast from `md` and stacked on phones. Text keeps its measure (lede and card text 62ch); the width goes to the cards and rows, not the line length. Quiet, skipped and failed days keep one column and have no glance.
- **Today at a glance** (`Glance`, `tally`). An `aside` under an ink rule, "From the 9 stories in your edition.", then three `BarList`s from the chart kit, each with its printed summary and its "Show the numbers" table, computed only from the stories this edition tells (its top takeaways and section rows, each once), so every reader's glance is their own: **Stories by stance** ("5 threats, 2 opportunities, 2 neutral"; each row wears its meaning token, threat/opportunity/neutral, and says it in words, the one place `BarList` rows carry their own colour, `BarItem.color`); **Who moved** (competitors by their stories' summed importance, "GitHub moved most: 2 stories, importance 132 in all.", each row a link to `/competitors/:slug`, JFrog never listed); **Your products in play** (the JFrog products the top stories name, by how many name each, "Artifactory is named in 3 of today's top stories.", each a link to `/products/:slug` once the product list has loaded). Lists show five rows (three on phones) before the rest fold into "Other (n)". A list with nothing to draw shows the kit's empty state; a day with no stories has no glance at all, never charts at zero.
- **Lede.** The headline (`display` serif, balanced, 30ch; 40ch past 90 characters, never smaller), the lead (`body` regular in `foreground`, 62ch), and the **funnel strip**: one `meta` line, "Scanned 412 › 61 relevant › 14 new signals · 6 in this briefing · 3 that matter", counts tabular and in foreground, the last in `primary-ink`. Chevrons join only the collection stages (each narrows the one before); what the briefing carries follows a dot, because a briefing can carry signals filed on earlier days. The counts are the DAY's (every run that filed a version of this date's briefing, summed), so they agree with Agent runs; the tooltip lists every stage, analysed signals included, and says where they come from ("From the 2 runs that filed this day's briefing"). It is text, not a bar: the stages span two orders of magnitude. A `primary-ink` "View agent run" link sits beside it.
- **Takeaway cards** (`TakeawayCard`). Up to three, one anatomy, editorial blocks rather than boxes: the serif `title` headline (`display` on the lead); its **weight** under it (`Weight`): the importance meter (the number, tabular, over a `meter` bar on its `meter-track`, 64px; on the lead the number at `title` over a 112px bar; one image named "Importance 88 of 100") and the **stance chip** (24px, 4px radius, the stance's icon and word in its meaning colour on its `-soft` ground with a 30% hairline; neutral on `card` with an `input` hairline), and on the lead only the competitor's 13-week **momentum** ("Momentum", the kit's `Sparkline` from `GET /competitors/overview`, "131 this week" printed beside it; left out while loading or when the competitor has no measured weeks); the **summary** (2–4 sentences copied from the story's analysis, `body` in `foreground`, 62ch); **why it matters** under its **angle label** (a `body` semibold label over the line, then the affected products as **product tags**: 24px, 4px radius, hairline, card ground, the product in `meta` medium and its effect as a `StanceLabel`, icon + word in the meaning colour, highest severity first, five at most then "+N more"; each tag opens its product page, with the chip's hover, an `input-hover` hairline on `accent`, and focus ring); **What to do** (a `body` semibold label over up to three actions in a muted-disc list; on the master edition each starts with its role in semibold, "Sales:", "Product:", "Executive:", "PMM & DevRel:"); then the foot: the **meta line** (`StoryMeta`, `meta`: the first-read mark when it applies, the trust line in its plain form, company chips, the outlets by name in `foreground` medium, the relative time) and **"Read full analysis →"** (`body` semibold `primary-ink`, always shown, never behind a hover). Blocks sit 20px apart, labels 4px over their text. The **lead** is the page's hero: it spans the stories column under the ink rule, its headline at `display`, its meter long and its momentum beside the chip; from `xl` its "What to do" takes a ruled right column (4fr : 3fr, `border-l`) beside the summary and why it matters, the middle row absorbing any difference in height so neither column leaves a hole. The **other two** sit beneath it on a hairline, two abreast from `2xl` split by a column rule, their feet aligned at the bottom; below `2xl` they stack full width, parted by hairlines. The lead reads first by position, width and its larger headline.
- **Angle labels.** Each card's why-it-matters line is labelled by where it comes from (`angle`, exact copy, the same as the email's): "Why it matters for JFrog" (the story's JFrog impact: every master card, an edition card whose analysis has no line for the role, and every row filed before angles, which carries no `angle`; an edition's stage-A card takes its role's own stage-A line under the role's label), "In your deals" (Sales: the first objection as "Objection: … — Response: …", else the first talk-track line as 'Say: "…"'), "Product angle" (the first assessment), "Strategic angle" (the first implication) and "Messaging angle" (the first messaging line). The label replaces "Why it matters for JFrog" in place, at its position, size and weight, so each edition reads as written for its role without another element on the card; the line it labels is not repeated in "What to do".
- **Exec risk chip.** On the Exec edition, a card whose analysis rates the risk to JFrog carries it beside the "Strategic angle" label, on the label's line (wrapping under it on a narrow column): a 20px mark, 4px radius, hairline, `meta` medium, reading "Risk: high", "Risk: medium" or "Risk: low" after the level's mark, the email's (up triangle, diamond, down triangle), so the level reads by shape and word, never colour alone. Only high takes the threat tone (`threat` on `threat-soft`, a `threat` hairline at 30%); medium is `foreground` and low `muted-foreground`, both on the card ground with an `input` hairline, as quiet as a product tag. It sits with the angle rather than on the meta line because the risk is the strategic angle's verdict (both come from the analysis's Executive section), while the meta line is the story's evidence (grade, trust, companies, outlet, time) and keeps one anatomy in every edition. The master never shows one, whatever a row carries.
- **One link per story.** A card (and a row) is a single link to `/signals/<id>` (`analysisHref(id)`: every story opens in the reader's own edition, a master fallback's too), named by its headline, with nothing interactive inside it: the trust line is text, chips are not filters, the time keeps only its hover tooltip. Native link behaviour works anywhere on the block (open in a new tab, middle-click), and each story is one Tab stop. The link is `isolate` and carries a `::before` wash that bleeds 12px past the text column (sideways only on rows, between their hairlines), so text stays aligned with the page: hover washes it in `accent` at 70% and underlines the headline, press deepens to full `accent`, keyboard focus draws the 2px `ring` around the wash. A **takeaway card** also links its product tags to the product pages, so its link is a **cover** (`story` `CARD`, `CARD_COVER`): the headline is the one link to the analysis, its `::after` spans the card 12px past it (the `relative isolate` card is its frame) and its `::before` is the wash behind the text; the product tags and the time sit above the cover (`relative z-10`), the tags links of their own beside it, never inside it, so hovering a tag lights the tag, not the card. A card is therefore its headline's Tab stop plus one per product tag (a story row stays one). The relative time is a deliberate dead spot: lifted above the cover so its tooltip stays reachable, a click on it opens nothing and the wash drops while it is hovered, the price of the exact time one hover away. On a card the headline underlines through the cover's own `hover:underline`; `STORY_TITLE`'s `group-hover/story:underline` serves the rows. A takeaway whose signal has been withdrawn reads the same but links nowhere and drops the affordance; its product tags still open their pages.
- **Where the body comes from.** A current briefing copies each card's summary, why it matters, products and actions from the story's verified analysis (`from_analysis`). One whose story had no current analysis at filing (thin, capped, failed, not yet written) carries the stage-A fact, impact and role lines and says why with the **first-read mark**: a 20px dashed `input`-bordered tag, the state's icon and title in `meta` medium, first on the meta line (never a warning tone). The reason is the story's LIVE analysis state (from the briefing's signal cards), in the analysis page's and the drawer's words from one copy source: "Full analysis pending", "Limited evidence: headline only" or "Short take: not analysed in full"; once the analysis has landed since filing, the mark goes. A briefing filed before analyses were attached to cards has only `so_what` and `now_what`: read as the summary and a single action, unmarked (those rows read `from_analysis` false whatever their source); its empty lines are left out, never shown as empty labels.
- **No story twice.** Every signal appears once per briefing: section rows drop any story the takeaways already tell, and the first mention wins across sections (older briefings repeated their takeaways in the sections; they read without the repeat).
- **Section heads and story rows.** A section head is `body` sans semibold on a 1px ink rule with its story count right ("2 stories", `meta`); its rows are hairline-separated, 16px padded, each one link to the story's page: the serif `title` headline, the **first sentence** of its summary (`body` muted, 62ch; a row filed before summaries existed reads its why-it-matters line), then the meta line (the first-read mark first when it applies, then stance, the plain trust line, company chips, outlet and relative time). A muted chevron at the row's end shows at rest and nudges on hover, with the wash and the headline underline. Each row leads with its importance meter in a 2.75rem gutter (as the Signals Explorer's); from `2xl` the meta line takes a 15rem column of its own at the row's right, so the rows use the width while the text keeps its measure. The sections run the stories column's full width.
- **Footer.** Watchlist (notes that link to their signal's analysis page, a muted chevron at rest as on the rows; the signal's headline beneath a note only when the story is not already a card or row on the page and the note does not quote it) and quiet competitors (chips with a one-line explanation), each under its own ink rule, two columns when both exist; a `meta` colophon closes the page ("Open any story for its full analysis: every source, and why it ranks where it does.").
- **States** (`BriefingStates`), each under an ink rule, calm, saying what happened and what to do: quiet ("No significant moves" as the headline, then "The last full briefing" as a serif link, then the quiet competitors); skipped at the budget (which cap, read from `/costs`, with its amounts: the total cap spent → only `LLM_TOTAL_BUDGET_USD` lifts it; otherwise the daily cap on the latest day → raise `LLM_DAILY_BUDGET_USD` or catch up with `--max-usd`; no figures, or an older day → both caps named, neither asserted); failed (signals carry over; "See the agent run"); no briefing yet (analysts: Run the pipeline; others: when it arrives); AI off ("Add OPENAI_API_KEY to enable AI analysis", or a bordered note above an older briefing); a missing date and load errors use `EmptyState`.

## 6. Do's and Don'ts

**Do**
- Lead each surface with what changed; keep raw detail one step behind (tooltips, drawers).
- Show source, time, matched evidence and health alongside every item.
- Use only token colors; use `primary-ink`, not `primary`, for green text and icons.
- Put the serif on headlines and story titles only; everything operational is Schibsted Grotesk.
- Pair every semantic color with a label or icon.
- Put floating layers on the semantic z-index scale; portaled content on `popover`.
- Honor reduced motion; ease out with the two named curves, never bounce.
- Show a signal's trust line (grade + trust state) wherever the signal appears. A quick look opens in the one app-level drawer by setting `?signal=<id>` (`components/signal/param`), never a second drawer; the whole story is the analysis page at `/signals/:id`, which the quick look links to ("Open full analysis →"). Build every link to that page with `analysisHref(id)`; it opens in the reader's own edition, as every page does.
- Start a Briefing section with a 1px ink rule; separate items with hairlines.
- Make a whole card or row ONE link with nothing interactive inside it, its affordance visible at rest and its hover, press and focus on a bleeding `::before` wash; a control that must stay a control belongs outside the link (a takeaway card's product tags: beside its cover link and above it, never nested).
- Use `meter`/`meter-track` for any value-against-maximum bar.
- Register every new `@theme` scale token with `cn`: `lib/utils` extends tailwind-merge with `THEME_SCALES` (the four sizes, `shadow-floating`, the two `ease-out-*` curves) so a size and a text colour coexist and two sizes dedupe; `lib/utils.test.ts` reads the `@theme` block and fails when a token is missing.
- Set every text size with one of the four: `text-display`, `text-title`, `text-body`, `text-meta`. Rank within a size with weight, colour, spacing and voice.

**Don't**
- No BI walls of charts, no rows of stat tiles, no hero-metric template. The ingestion funnel is one 8px bar per table row; the agent funnel is a line of text; the spend has one chart (the week) and two meters. A chart from the kit answers its section's question under a printed summary; it is never decoration and never one of a grid.
- Don't hand-build a chart outside `components/charts`, and don't give a chart a colour outside its roles: no series in a meaning token, no sixth hue (fold into "Other"), no line drawn between reported figures, no second value axis, no zero drawn for data that was never measured.
- No side-stripe colored borders, gradient text, decorative glass, or eyebrow labels above sections. A takeaway or headline never gets a small label above it ("Top takeaways" is a screen-reader heading).
- Don't reuse the `funnel-*` ramp outside `FunnelBar`; it means pipeline stage.
- Don't hide a meaning in color: stance, trust state, verification, health and an Exec card's risk level are always icon plus word.
- No cream, sand or beige backgrounds; neutrals stay green-tinted.
- No shadows on in-flow surfaces; no hard offset shadows.
- No serif in tables, forms, buttons or nav.
- Don't apply tabular numerals to whole text blocks.
- Don't hide what is clickable behind a hover: on a block or row whose whole area is the target (briefing cards and rows, Explorer and Search everything rows, watchlist notes), an underline or chevron that only appears on hover is not an affordance; they show a resting cue. Inline title links inside a list (Sources, related signals, Feed titles) are conventional links and are not meant.
- No fifth size: no new `--text-*` token, no `text-[…]` size, no Tailwind default size, not even behind a breakpoint. A lead item is never set larger than its siblings; give it width or position instead.

## Divergence from the seed and uncanonized items

- The seed's Morning Briefing is built: editorial takeaways, Admiralty grades and approval state are shipped patterns, recorded above. The Signals Explorer is built on `components/signal` and the meter tokens. Agent runs (`/admin/agents`, `/admin/agents/:id`) is built on the same tokens, with the ink-rule section grammar and the meters; `PlaceholderPage` was retired then, held the competitor and product deep-dive routes again from the competitor list, held only the product routes once the competitor deep dive shipped, and is retired since the product pages shipped: `features/placeholder` keeps only the not-found page.
- The competitor and product pages' rules (JFrog's products and their order, the severity weights, the overlap, threat, trend and brief thresholds, the weekly windows and the collected-since day) are read from `GET /intel-reference` (`lib/intelReference`: the read, `RulesContext`/`useRules` for a page and its sections, `useJfrogProducts` for a product name on any page); the earlier hard-coded copies of `JFROG_PRODUCTS` and the config thresholds are gone, and `lib/intelReference.test.ts` fails on a new one. A product name links to its page once the list has loaded, and is text until then.
- The `chart-*` tokens were defined but unused until the chart kit, which retuned them (§2 Chart series) and is their only consumer. The 7-day spend chart predates the kit and stays as built (`meter` columns in HTML).
- The analysis page replaced the `/signals/:id` redirect to the drawer, and the drawer became a quick look. Its main-column section heads and the drawer's are the serif at `title`; the side column's heads stay `body` semibold on hairlines, read as labels of a secondary column rather than sections of the analysis, so the column does not compete. The drawer header's stance · type · scope line moved below the title (no label above a headline).
- The Briefing's cards were rebuilt: the title-only button that opened the drawer, the so-what and now-what lines, the lead's 15rem margin column and the per-signal source buttons gave way to one link per story with the analysis's summary, why it matters with product tags, up to three actions and a visible "Read full analysis →"; stories open the full page, not the quick look.
- Fixed: before the fix, `cn` was plain tailwind-merge and dropped a role size beside a text colour, so badges, run and health badges, tooltips, table heads, trust lines, company chips, the funnel strip, `xs` buttons and empty-state titles rendered at the inherited size (and an `xs` link button lost its colour). They now render at the sizes recorded above.
- The four sizes replaced an eight-role scale (caption 12, meta 13, ui 14, body 16, lede 18, headline 20, title 28, display 44, plus 17px one-offs for row titles), mapped: caption → meta, ui and lede → body, headline → title, title → display for page and briefing headlines and → title for card headlines; the 17px row titles became `title`. The original `body` 16/26 is 15/24 here, because one size serves both running text and controls.
