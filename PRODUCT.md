# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is the people who consume competitive intelligence across JFrog: Sales and Solution Engineers preparing for deals, Product Managers tracking competitor releases and capability gaps, executives following strategic moves, and Product Marketing and Developer Relations watching messaging and community buzz. They open FrogEye at a desk on a laptop or monitor, usually at the start of the day or right before a meeting, and want to know in minutes what changed and what it means for them.

The secondary audience is the Competitive Intelligence analyst team that curates the system: they review AI-drafted battlecards, comparison changes and exec briefs, manage sources, and keep the intelligence trustworthy. Their tools matter, but they serve the consumer experience.

Phones get a readable briefing only; every other surface is designed for desktop.

## Product Purpose

FrogEye keeps JFrog updated daily on the latest news and insights across its industry and shows how JFrog compares to its main competitors. It collects public market signals every day, uses AI agents to turn them into ranked, evidence-backed insight, and publishes a Morning Briefing tailored to each role, alongside a living JFrog-vs-competitors comparison and battlecards that an analyst approves before they reach the field.

Success looks like each team reading its briefing every morning, trusting what it says because every claim links to its source, and acting on it: a rep walks into a call knowing the competitor's latest move, a PM sees a capability gap the day it opens, an executive spots a strategic shift before it becomes a surprise.

FrogEye covers competitors and the industry, never JFrog's own news: JFrog appears only as the subject of "why it matters for JFrog" and of what each team should do, on every surface and for every role.

## Positioning

FrogEye tells each JFrog team what changed in the market today and what to do about it, with evidence.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, calm, trustworthy. FrogEye speaks like a seasoned analyst briefing a busy colleague: direct, unhurried, never alarmist, and always able to show where a claim came from. Confidence comes from evidence and clarity, not from volume or decoration.

Reference points: the editorial briefing craft of the FT and The Economist Espresso (clear hierarchy, strong typography, every item answering "why it matters"), and the clarity and polish of the Stripe Dashboard (trustworthy, precise presentation of data).

## Anti-references

- BI walls of charts: Tableau or Power BI style dashboards crammed with charts and widgets competing for attention.
- Generic SaaS admin templates: cards everywhere, rows of stat tiles, repeated icon-plus-heading grids.

## Design Principles

1. **So-what first.** Every surface leads with what changed and what to do about it; raw data and detail sit one step behind.
2. **Every claim shows its evidence.** Sources, confidence and approval state are always visible or one hover away; trust is earned on screen, not asserted.
3. **Calm over loud.** Urgency is reserved for what is truly urgent; hierarchy, not decoration, tells the reader where to look.
4. **Built for the reader.** Optimize for the consumer skimming before a meeting; analyst power tools stay out of their way.
5. **Showcase where it counts.** The Morning Briefing is the flagship and should feel crafted like a top-tier editorial product, while the rest stays a disciplined, efficient tool.
